COVER LETTER TO THE EDITORS
Transactions of the International Society for Music Information Retrieval (TISMIR)

Manuscript title:
  "Probing What Music Classifiers Actually Learn: A Multi-Method Explainability
   Analysis of Genre Classification on GTZAN"

Submitted by: J. Abimbola

------------------------------------------------------------------------
AIM OF THE PUBLICATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------

This manuscript presents a systematic, multi-method explainability analysis of a
convolutional neural network (CNN) trained on the GTZAN music genre dataset. The
central research question is whether CNN-based genre classifiers attend to
musically meaningful spectral features or instead exploit dataset-specific
artifacts such as silence patterns, recording noise, and artist-fingerprinting
effects.

To address this, we apply three complementary XAI techniques — SHapley Additive
exPlanations (SHAP), Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME), and
CLS-token attention visualisation from a fine-tuned Audio Spectrogram Transformer
(AST) — to the same subject model and introduce a four-metric quantitative
evaluation framework:

  1. Music-informed spectral alignment (Spearman rho between observed and
     expected frequency-band attribution rankings per genre)
  2. Faithfulness under progressive pixel masking (confidence-drop curves)
  3. Inter-method agreement (Pearson r, Spearman rho, top-10% IoU between SHAP
     and LIME attribution maps)
  4. Spurious correlation detection (silence-to-high-energy attribution ratio)

Findings demonstrate strong genre dependence: hip-hop exhibits near-perfect
positive alignment with low-frequency musical priors (rho = 0.95), while country
and pop show inverse alignment, elevated silence attribution, and low inter-method
agreement. SHAP and LIME systematically disagree for the same predictions
(Pearson r range: -0.01 to 0.20; top-10% IoU range: 6.0%–18.8%), confirming the
XAI "disagreement problem" in the MIR domain for the first time with quantitative
metrics.

The contribution is methodological: a reproducible, domain-grounded evaluation
pipeline for explanation quality in music classification research that motivates
multi-method triangulation as standard MIR practice.
